The gap between a network on paper and one that works.

Have you done this before at this scale?
Wireless · Ericsson

Ericsson's MOAI in-building rollout was projected at four months. With Meritech's automated drive test pipeline handling log upload, processing, and report generation, the project completed in one month. 17,000 sites. 100% automation. Zero manual intervention. Standardized global KPIs across every site and region.

Nokia, NTT, and Rakuten are among the operators whose networks Meritech's engineers have measured, analyzed, and tuned. The fieldwork is real. The references are named.

What does working with us actually look like?
Wireless · Field service

A Meritech service engagement runs in three phases — planning, field testing, optimization — and the data from each phase feeds directly into the next. No format translation between stages. No institutional knowledge gap between the team that planned and the team that tests.

Engineers show up with Sigma-LA in the van, Sigma-AQ running the automated pipeline, Sigma-PA doing the analysis, and Centra-SD consolidating everything. The deliverable at the end is a structured report with KPIs, root cause findings, and optimization recommendations — not a raw data dump.

Why Meritech and not someone else
Wireless · Instruments

Most wireless services firms bring third-party instruments into the field. Meritech's engineers operate the instruments their own team designed — Sigma-LA, Sigma-AQ, Sigma-PA, Centra-SD. The tool and the team share a codebase, a data format, and a standard.

When something behaves unexpectedly in the field, the engineer running the test can reach the engineer who wrote the firmware. That feedback loop doesn't exist anywhere else in this market.

Three phases.
One data pipeline.

Planning decisions feed the drive test. Drive test data feeds the optimization. Each phase hands the next a structured input — not a gap to fill from scratch. The engineer who analyzed the propagation model is the same one reviewing the drive test KPIs.

Phase 1 · Planning & Design
Before a single antenna goes up.

Site selection, propagation modelling, capacity planning, and frequency optimization. Engineers analyse terrain, buildings, and vegetation to identify strategic sites. Coverage gaps, shadow zones, and interference areas are found and solved on paper — not in the field.
