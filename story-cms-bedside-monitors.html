CMS — Central Monitoring System for Bedside Monitors

A major medical equipment manufacturer needed one station that could communicate with up to 32 bedside monitors at once, collecting data with millisecond latency and displaying vital signs, alarms, and historical records for audit. When the data is a patient's heartbeat, "fast enough" has a very precise definition.

Objective

Thirty-two heartbeats. One screen. Zero lag.

The system had to communicate with up to 32 bedside monitors simultaneously, collect data with millisecond latency, and present it usably: live vital signs on screen, alarms when a reading crosses a threshold, and historical records retained for audit purposes. A central monitoring station is only as good as its slowest refresh — a delayed alarm is a missed alarm.

Approach & Solution

Parallel by design. Fluid by necessity.

01
Performance-oriented architecture with parallel processing

The architecture was built around parallel processing from the start — 32 monitors streaming vitals concurrently can't be handled one at a time, so every channel gets processed simultaneously to hold the millisecond latency target.

02
Vital statistics replay on graphs

Any patient's vital statistics can be replayed on graphs for medical analysis — clinicians scroll back through exactly what the monitor saw, when it saw it, instead of reconstructing events from memory and notes.

03
Responsive, fluid GUI and dashboard displays

The GUI and dashboards were engineered to stay responsive and fluid under full load — because a monitoring screen that stutters doesn't just annoy, it erodes the reliability and interpretability the ward depends on.

Business Value

Seconds saved at the station are seconds saved at the bed.

Enhanced nurse response times — real-time displays and alarm features surface deterioration the moment it happens, wherever the nurse is standing.
Centralized monitoring — one station watches every bed, improving patient care accessibility across the ward instead of relying on room-by-room rounds.
Faster clinical analysis in emergencies — replay functionality lets clinicians rewind the vitals and see what led up to an event, while the event is still unfolding.
Audit-ready history — historical records of vitals and alarms are retained for audit purposes, documenting the care every patient received.
