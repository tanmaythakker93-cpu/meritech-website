Surface Wear Monitoring IoT Device

A leading industrial pipe manufacturer needed to monitor the wear of the internal surface of their pipes — without cutting them open. Meritech built an IoT device that measures ceramic coating thickness by ultrasound, using a 5MHz probe with WiFi connectivity, reporting into a cloud dashboard that warns before wear becomes failure.

Objective Approach & Solution Business Value Technologies Used
Objective

See the wear inside the pipe — while it's still in service.

Industrial pipes carrying abrasive material are lined with ceramic coating, and that coating wears down invisibly from the inside. The client needed an IoT device to monitor the wear of the internal surface of the pipe in place: measuring ceramic coating thickness via ultrasound with a 5MHz probe, connected over WiFi, and delivering the readings to a backend cloud dashboard where wear becomes a trend you can see coming — not a rupture you discover.

Approach & Solution

Ultrasound physics, FPGA speed, cloud visibility.

01
Embedded hardware on the XENO+ WiFi+BLE ML SOM

The device's embedded hardware was designed around Meritech's own XENO+ WiFi+BLE ML system-on-module — connectivity and edge intelligence proven on the module, so the custom engineering focused on the ultrasound measurement chain.

02
Embedded software components

Device firmware handles the measurement cycle end to end — driving the probe, capturing the echo, computing thickness, and shipping the result over WiFi to the cloud.

03
FPGA RTL code on a Lattice FPGA

Ultrasound echoes at 5MHz move faster than a microcontroller can sample them. Custom RTL code on a Lattice FPGA handles the high-speed signal path, giving the device lab-instrument timing in an industrial package.

04
Cloud dashboard with historical time series

A dashboard application built on a customizable platform displays pipe thickness and temperature data as historical time-series graphs — so wear rate, not just wear state, is visible at a glance.

Business Value

Predictive maintenance, sold with every pipe.

Early warning notifications — alerts fire when ceramic coating thickness drops below critical thresholds, before wear turns into downtime or failure.
Real-time IoT digitization — the manufacturer's industrial pipe offerings gained a live digital layer, differentiating a traditionally passive product.
Predictive maintenance for end-customers — pipe buyers plan replacements from measured wear trends instead of guesswork or fixed schedules.
Thickness and temperature history — historical time-series graphs turn every pipe into a documented asset with a measurable service life.
5MHz

Ultrasound probe frequency — precise enough to measure a ceramic lining's thickness from outside the pipe, while the pipe stays in service.
